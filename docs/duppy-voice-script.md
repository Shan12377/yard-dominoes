# Duppy voice — recording script

You are the voice. These are the moments the game can actually trigger, taken
from the events the engine emits, so every line here has somewhere real to go.

**The words below are starting points, not a script to read.** You know how
this sounds in a yard and I don't. If a line reads stiff, say it your way —
your phrasing is the asset, and the whole point is that this cannot be
faked by anyone who has not sat at the table.

---

## Record one character, not five

There are five duppy levels, but one person cannot voice five opponents
without every table sounding like the same woman talking to herself. So:
**one character, done properly.** She takes one seat; the other duppies stay
silent for now. That is natural — in any real yard one person is the loud one
and the rest just play.

Pick a name you'd actually call her. `Miss Ivy`, `Tanty`, `Auntie V` — one
word or two, something a player will repeat when they tell somebody they got
beaten by her. Tell me what you pick and I'll wire it in.

Her personality should be **sharp but warm** — the auntie who takes your money
and enjoys it. Not cruel. A player who loses to her should want a rematch, not
feel got at. That single decision matters more than any individual line.

---

## How to record

- **Phone voice memo is fine.** A quiet room with soft things in it — a bed,
  curtains, a closet with clothes — beats an empty room every time. Avoid
  kitchens and bathrooms.
- Hold the phone about a hand's width away, slightly off to the side so your
  breath doesn't hit it directly.
- **Highest quality setting**, and don't send them through WhatsApp — it
  compresses them badly. AirDrop, email, or a cable.
- **Leave two full seconds of silence between each take**, so they can be cut
  apart cleanly.
- Do it all in **one sitting** if you can. Voices shift between days and the
  clips will sound like different people.
- At the end, record **thirty seconds of you saying nothing** in the same
  room. That's room tone, and it makes every edit invisible.
- **Say each line three or four times**, differently. Bored, sharp, laughing.
  I'll keep the best and it costs you nothing to have options.

**Keep them short.** Most of these should land under a second and a half.
They play over a live game, so a long line steps on the next move.

**Two things to avoid:** don't name specific tiles or numbers — she can't know
which tile is coming. And don't assume the player is a man; most lines should
work either way.

## The lines

Counts matter more than wording. Anything that fires several times a hand
needs enough variants that nobody hears the same clip twice in one sitting.

### When you pass — the best moment in the game (5 lines)

She knows exactly what your pass just told her. This is the needle, and it
should feel like being read.

> "Mm hmm." · "So yuh nuh have none?" · "Noted." · "Aight, aight, mi see yuh."
> · a single small laugh

### When she passes (4 lines)

Caught out. Annoyed at herself, never at you.

> "Cho." · "Mi cyaan play." · "Mm." · a suck-teeth

### When she slams a double (5 lines)

The big one. This is the sound people will screenshot the app for.

> "Boom!" · "Tek dat!" · "Yes!" · "Right deh so." · "Haaa!"

### When she cuts you off (4 lines)

She just closed the end you needed.

> "Not today." · "Mi did a wait pon dat." · "Yuh see it?" · "Mm hmm."

### When she plays out and wins (5 lines)

> "Dat done." · "Game." · "Tell dem." · "Easy." · a warm laugh

### When she wins a blocked hand on count (4 lines)

Different feeling — she won by counting, not by playing out. Quieter, smug.

> "Count it." · "Mi did a count." · "Lickle bit less." · "Mm hmm."

### When she loses (4 lines)

**Record these generously.** A duppy who takes a loss well is what makes her
likeable, and likeable is what brings players back.

> "Yuh get me dat time." · "Awright, awright." · "Nice play, man." ·
> a genuine laugh

### When she poses to open the hand (3 lines)

> "Mi a start." · "Come we go." · "Sit down, den."

### When she draws from the boneyard (3 lines)

> "Gimme one." · "Mm." · a small sigh

### When she's down to her last tile (3 lines)

The warning shot. Should make the player nervous.

> "One left." · "Mi nearly done." · "Watch mi now."

### Six love against the player (3 lines)

The heaviest moment in Jamaican dominoes. Big, but still warm — she should
sound delighted, not vicious.

> "SIX LOVE!" · "Six love, man!" · a long laugh

### Six love against her (2 lines)

She has to take it too. Being able to lose the big one is what makes her real.

> "Lawd." · "Alright. Mi deserve dat."

### When the score bruks back to nothing (3 lines)

> "All a dat gone." · "Back to zero." · a suck-teeth

### When the player makes a genuinely good move (3 lines)

The coach can tell when you played the best available move. Her noticing is
worth more than any badge.

> "Ooh." · "Dat good, yuh know." · "Mm! Where yuh learn dat?"

### When the player takes too long (4 lines)

Gentle, never nagging.

> "Yuh sleeping?" · "Any day now." · "Tek yuh time." · a hum

---

**That's about sixty clips, and it's roughly forty-five minutes of work.**

Don't try to be perfect. The takes where you laugh at your own line are
usually the ones worth keeping.

## Naming the files

One line per file, so nothing has to be guessed at:

```
ivy-pass-them-01.wav
ivy-pass-them-02.wav
ivy-slam-01.wav
ivy-sixlove-us-01.wav
```

Use the section names above as the middle part and number the takes. If a file
name is wrong I can rename it; what I cannot recover is a single long
recording with everything in it, so keep them separate.

---

## What happens after

These are pre-recorded clips played on game events. They have nothing to do
with the live WebRTC voice mesh and cannot destabilise it — a missing or
broken clip plays nothing at all and the game carries on.

Muting her must be one tap and must stick between sessions. Some people play
in bed at two in the morning, and a voice they can't switch off is an
uninstall.
